Commercializing a New Distribution Channel

Channel Strategy Near-Zero CAC Tri-Party Framework Portfolio Diversification Building-Level Exclusivity

Designed and operationalized a tri-party distribution model with residential complex operators, enabling on-site activation and management of home fiber broadband at preferential rates — increasing market share, defending installed base from price-based competition, and establishing a near-zero CAC acquisition channel.

Context
Ooredoo Qatar is the incumbent telecom operator defending against the challenger Vodafone Qatar over market share in the mobile and fixed consumer segments.
Problem
As the incumbent operator, Ooredoo Qatar faced pressure from aggressive discounting in the residential fixed broadband segment, resulting in declining market share in communities where tenants made connectivity decisions independently at move-in, without incentive to favor Ooredoo.
Distribution Strategy
Traditional acquisition channels — direct sales, retail, telemarketing, digital — all reached the tenant after move-in, when the connectivity decision was already made and competitors could win the conversation through discounting. The real commercial opportunity was upstream: the leasing workflow itself was an untapped distribution surface where the connectivity decision could be set before the tenant ever shopped. Shifting acquisition to that point in the customer journey removed Ooredoo from the price-comparison conversation entirely and replaced discount-driven competition with a structural advantage no challenger could replicate without owning the same partner relationships.
Business Case & Economics
Traditional fixed-broadband CAC carried a fully loaded cost across acquisition marketing, retail footprint, telemarketing, sales commissions, and installation visits. Embedded distribution collapsed that load to near-zero incremental CAC per tenant — the landlord relationship was the only sales cost, amortized across hundreds of units in each building. Retention economics were equally compelling: tenants activated through the leasing flow stayed with Ooredoo for the duration of their lease, with minimal churn-trigger events, and building-level long-term exclusivity compounded the value of every landlord won. The economic rationale was clean: invest in partner-side enablement now, earn a structurally lower CAC and structurally higher retention for the life of every building contract.
Strategic Portfolio Role
Embedded distribution was not a tactical promotion — it became Ooredoo’s fifth acquisition channel, alongside direct sales, retail, telemarketing, and digital. Its strategic role inside the portfolio was growth diversification: a channel that performed on a different economic model, was insulated from the same competitive pressures, and reduced the company’s dependency on traditional acquisition channels increasingly squeezed by challenger pricing. Adding the channel rebalanced the commercial mix and made the overall acquisition engine structurally more resilient.
Proposition
Partner with property managers to embed Ooredoo into the leasing process, design a tri-party framework that gives landlords a revenue-generating tool, tenants an effortless move-in experience, and Ooredoo a zero-cost customer acquisition channel.
Landlords
Access to bulk-rate services to create competitive rental offerings. A self-service digital tool to activate or deactivate services, eliminating uncompensated utility costs.
Tenants
Immediate, hassle-free connectivity at move-in at discounted rates. Full flexibility to upgrade with personal add-ons, speed boosts, TV channel via the Ooredoo consumer app.
Ooredoo
Exclusive long-term contracts effectively blocking competitor entry and securing a zero-cost customer acquisition channel with predictable long-term revenue.
Embedded Distribution Strategy: Identified the precise points in the leasing workflow where Ooredoo could be embedded to create a zero-cost acquisition trigger — designing a tri-party distribution model that made connectivity activation a natural part of tenant move-in rather than an independent post-move purchasing decision. Front-end simplicity was paramount; all technical complexity was abstracted through back-end integration to preserve the partner-led commercial experience.
Commercial Bundling & Billing Model: Designed a hybrid commercial model with CRM, Billing, and IT teams that cleanly separated base charges to the landlord from tenant-level charges. This preserved Ooredoo's competitive advantage while ensuring partner profitability — and integrated fully with existing BSS/OSS systems with no disruption to desktop operations.
Consumer App Integration & Upsell Channel: Defined and drove requirements to extend the platform to B2B2C-activated subscribers — enabling self-service plan management, usage visibility, bill payment, and support. Once activated through the landlord, tenants could independently upgrade to higher speeds, TV channels, and add-ons through the Ooredoo app, creating a self-serve upsell channel with no incremental sales cost.
Channel Rollout & Partner Activation: Developed co-branded partner playbooks, commercial term sheets, and onboarding kits. Sequenced the channel rollout across landlord segments, implemented a dedicated account management model to sustain and grow each B2B partner relationship, and built a structured training program for the B2B sales team ahead of launch to ensure consistent partner activation at scale.

Self-service activation and management for property operators


Launch Readiness & Partner Enablement
Drove cross-functional launch readiness across every function that touched the channel:
  • Sales readiness — B2B account managers trained on partner pitch, commercial terms, objection handling, and co-selling motion with property operators
  • Partner onboarding — standardized landlord activation kit (contract, co-branded collateral, portal credentials, training session) compressing time-to-first-tenant
  • Operational readiness — provisioning flows, field-install scheduling, and landlord-side controls validated end-to-end before any building went live
  • Billing readiness — separation of landlord base charges and tenant-level charges configured and tested in BSS/OSS, with reconciliation reports for partner finance teams
  • Consumer app readiness — B2B2C-activated tenants flowed cleanly into the Ooredoo app for self-serve plan management, upsell, and support from day one
  • Escalation & support processes — tiered escalation paths spanning landlord-side issues, tenant-side issues, and shared edge cases, with named owners in each function
Competitive Advantage
The channel produced a structural competitive position that pricing alone could not replicate. Building-level exclusivity meant every landlord won was a closed market for the lifetime of the contract — tenants in those properties became Ooredoo customers by default, removing them from the price-comparison set. The model reduced exposure to price wars because the buying decision happened inside the leasing flow, not on a competitor’s comparison page. And the partner relationships themselves acted as a structural barrier to entry: a challenger would have to displace Ooredoo landlord-by-landlord, against incumbency, integration, and proven economics — a much harder play than running a discount campaign.
Outcomes
  • Acquired customers at near-zero CAC via embedded B2B2C distribution
  • Captured +2% market share in residential fiber within Year 1
  • Onboarded 100+ property operators protecting a 500K+ subscriber base from challenger pricing
  • Locked in exclusive long-term contracts blocking competitor entry at the building level
  • Created a self-serve upsell channel via the consumer app — growing ARPA with no incremental sales cost
  • Established embedded distribution as a repeatable acquisition model that could be extended to future partner ecosystems beyond residential property

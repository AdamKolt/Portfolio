CCTV-as-a-Service

ARPA Expansion Adjacent Revenue Managed Services CAPEX-to-OPEX SMB Commercialization

Designed and built CCTV-as-a-Service, a managed security offering for Ooredoo’s SMB base that converts one-time hardware spend into a recurring monthly subscription bundled with connectivity. The commercial model, partner ecosystem, technical architecture, and go-to-market are validated and launch-ready — positioning the offer to lift ARPA, deepen account tenure, and seed a broader SMB managed-services portfolio. Currently awaiting Ministry of Interior approval before commercial launch.

Context
Ooredoo’s SMB segment faced two structural pressures: connectivity ARPU was flat, and customers were buying adjacent technology — surveillance, security, IT services — from fragmented third-party vendors with no operational support. The strategic mandate was clear: extend the customer relationship beyond connectivity into recurring managed services that lift ARPA, deepen stickiness, and crowd out point-solution competitors. The SMB CCTV market was the highest-value adjacency: large addressable spend, fragmented supply, and a customer base that already trusts Ooredoo for mission-critical connectivity.
Strategic Frame
The commercial thesis: SMBs do not want to buy cameras — they want peace of mind, evidence on demand, and predictable cost. The right product is not a hardware sale but a fully managed subscription that bundles cameras, installation, cloud retention, maintenance, and support into a single monthly fee on the existing Ooredoo invoice. That framing converts a high-friction CAPEX decision into a low-friction OPEX one, attaches naturally to the connectivity bundle, and positions Ooredoo as the single-vendor security partner for the SMB segment.
1

Commercial Model — CAPEX-to-OPEX with ARPA Mechanics

Designed a tiered subscription model priced by camera count, retention window, and service level — mapped directly to SMB sub-segments (retail, F&B, professional services, multi-site operators). Anchored pricing to the monthly OPEX equivalent of a 36-month CAPEX purchase plus ongoing storage and maintenance, then layered margin into the managed-service wrap rather than the hardware. Built the offer to attach to the existing connectivity bill, so every CCTV sale flows through the same billing relationship, the same account manager, and the same renewal cycle — lifting ARPA without adding billing complexity for the customer.

2

Partner Ecosystem & Technical Architecture

Structured the supply side as a partner-led delivery model: licensed surveillance vendors for hardware import, installation, and field maintenance; Ooredoo for connectivity, cloud storage, billing, customer relationship, and service orchestration. Negotiated partner commercial terms that protected Ooredoo margin while giving partners predictable recurring volume. Designed the technical architecture around cloud-stored footage with policy-based retention, role-based access controls, and a mobile-first owner experience — so multi-site SMB operators can view all locations from a single pane, on the device they already carry. The architecture is vendor-agnostic at the camera layer, which keeps the offer resilient to hardware supply changes.

3

Go-to-Market & Operational Readiness

Built the launch motion around Ooredoo’s existing SMB channels: account managers carry the offer into renewal and expansion conversations, retail stores demo it to walk-in business customers, and the digital self-service channel handles single-site low-touch purchases. Designed installation and service workflows that integrate cleanly with Ooredoo’s existing field operations and SMB support tiers — no parallel ops org required. Sales enablement, pricing tools, contract templates, partner SLAs, and customer onboarding flows are complete. The offer is operationally ready to ship the moment regulatory clearance closes.

Status & Outcome
  • Commercial model, partner agreements, technical stack, and GTM motion validated and launch-ready.
  • Currently awaiting Ministry of Interior approval for the managed-service commercial model — a regulatory step specific to surveillance categories in-market.
  • Positioned to lift SMB ARPA, extend account tenure, and seed a broader managed-services portfolio (alarm/access, IT services, business Wi-Fi) once the regulatory gate clears.

CCTV as a Service

ARPA Expansion Adjacent Revenue Managed Services CAPEX-to-OPEX Retention Mechanism

Designed and built CCTV-as-a-Service, a managed security offering for Ooredoo’s SMB base that converts one-time hardware spend into a recurring monthly subscription bundled with connectivity.

Context
Connectivity alone was becoming a harder lever for growth. While SMB customers relied on Ooredoo for mission-critical connectivity, they were purchasing surveillance, security, and other technology services from a fragmented ecosystem of local vendors. The strategic question was how to expand the customer relationship beyond connectivity into higher-value recurring services. CCTV stood out as the most compelling adjacency: large customer spend, fragmented supply, natural connectivity attachment, and a commercial model that could be reimagined from a one-time hardware purchase into a recurring managed-service subscription.
Strategic Frame
SMBs do not want to buy cameras — they want compliance, peace of mind, evidence on demand, and predictable cost. The right product is a fully managed subscription that bundles cameras, installation, cloud retention, maintenance, and support into a single monthly fee on the existing Ooredoo invoice. That framing converts a high-friction CAPEX decision into a low-friction OPEX one, attaches naturally to the connectivity bundle, and positions Ooredoo as the single-vendor security partner for the SMB segment.
1

Business Case & Revenue Model

The SMB surveillance opportunity in-market was sized in the tens of thousands of addressable sites with substantial annual spend across hardware, installation, and storage. Modeled the offer as a tiered monthly subscription per site, scaling with camera count, retention window, and service level. The revenue model replaced one-time hardware margin with multi-year recurring revenue with markup. The subscription structure also compounded ARPA with every renewal cycle, against a hardware-resale model where revenue resets to zero the day the box ships.

2

Target Segments & Use Cases

The launch segments were chosen for surveillance density, regulatory exposure, and existing Ooredoo-customer overlap:retail, hospitality (F&B, hotels), professional services & clinics, and multi-site SMB operators. Across every segment, the offer was scoped to address surveillance and compliance requirements the customer already faced — not to upsell capability the customer did not need.

3

Portfolio Expansion Strategy

The opportunity was larger than CCTV itself. Ooredoo already offered managed services such as managed routers and network security, proving that SMB customers were willing to consume technology as a service rather than as a one-time purchase. CCTV extended that model into physical security — a large adjacent category with strong attachment to connectivity and a fragmented competitive landscape. The commercial framework was intentionally designed to be repeatable, combining subscription pricing, partner-led delivery, and a single customer relationship. Beyond surveillance, the same model could be extended into adjacent services such as access control, alarm monitoring, and other managed workplace solutions, allowing Ooredoo to expand wallet share while leveraging existing commercial and operational capabilities.

4

Commercial Model — CAPEX-to-OPEX with ARPA Mechanics

Designed a tiered subscription model priced by camera count, retention window, and service level — mapped directly to SMB sub-segments (retail, F&B, professional services, multi-site operators). Anchored pricing to the monthly OPEX equivalent of a 36-month CAPEX purchase plus ongoing storage and maintenance, then layered margin into the managed-service wrap rather than the hardware. Built the offer to attach to the existing connectivity bill, so every CCTV sale flows through the same billing relationship, the same account manager, and the same renewal cycle — lifting ARPA without adding billing complexity for the customer.

5

Delivery Ecosystem & Service Model

Structured the supply side as a partner-led delivery model: licensed surveillance vendors deliver hardware, installation, and field maintenance; Ooredoo owns connectivity, the customer relationship, billing, and service orchestration. Negotiated partner economics that protected Ooredoo margin while giving partners predictable recurring volume — a sustainable commercial split that aligns both sides to long-term customer retention rather than one-off hardware sales. The service-delivery design — standard onboarding workflows, SLA-backed response, consolidated single-pane visibility for multi-site operators — is built on Ooredoo’s existing field-operations and SMB support tiers, so the offer scales without standing up a parallel ops organization. The result is operational scalability across site count and segment, and commercial resilience against hardware-supply or single-vendor risk because partner substitution does not break the customer experience.

6

Go-to-Market & Operational Readiness

Built the launch motion around Ooredoo's existing SMB channels: account managers carry the offer into renewal and expansion conversations, retail stores demonstrate it to walk-in business customers, and digital channels support low-touch acquisition. Developed pricing tools, contract templates, partner onboarding processes, sales enablement materials, customer onboarding journeys, and SLA governance required for launch. Commercial, operational, and partner readiness activities are complete, positioning the offer for immediate market release upon regulatory approval.

7

Launch Governance & Regulatory Readiness

The program was governed through a single cross-functional commercialization forum that brought together Product, Legal, Regulatory Affairs, Sales, Operations, and Partner Management. Rather than allowing each team to run independently, every function carried clear launch-readiness deliverables and reported progress against agreed milestones. As I owned the offer design and pricing model; Legal managed customer and partner agreements; Sales prepared channel readiness and enablement; Operations stood up installation, service, and support processes; and Partner Management onboarded licensed vendors and finalized service-level commitments. Regulatory led engagement with the Ministry of Interior, managing the approval process required for surveillance-related services.

Commercialization Readiness Outcomes
  • Commercial model validated — subscription tiers, segment fit, and unit economics signed off against the business case
  • Pricing approved — tiered monthly pricing approved across SMB sub-segments, with attach incentives mapped to the connectivity bundle
  • Partner agreements complete — licensed surveillance vendors contracted on commercial terms, SLAs, and recurring-volume commitments
  • Sales readiness complete — enablement, battlecards, pricing tools, contract templates, and channel quotas in place across field, retail, and digital
  • Operational readiness complete — installation, provisioning, billing, service, and support workflows tested end-to-end on existing SMB ops infrastructure
  • Regulatory submission completed — full Ministry of Interior submission filed for the managed-service commercial model; clearance pending

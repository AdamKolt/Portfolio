Transforming Hardware Sales into Recurring Revenue

ARPA Expansion Adjacent Revenue Managed Services CAPEX-to-OPEX SMB Commercialization

Designed and built CCTV-as-a-Service, a managed security offering for Ooredoo’s SMB base that converts one-time hardware spend into a recurring monthly subscription bundled with connectivity. The commercial model, partner ecosystem, technical architecture, and go-to-market are validated and launch-ready — positioning the offer to lift ARPA, deepen account tenure, and seed a broader SMB managed-services portfolio. Currently awaiting Ministry of Interior approval before commercial launch.

Context
Ooredoo’s SMB segment faced two structural pressures: connectivity ARPU was flat, and customers were buying adjacent technology — surveillance, security, IT services — from fragmented third-party vendors with no operational support. The strategic mandate was clear: extend the customer relationship beyond connectivity into recurring managed services that lift ARPA, deepen stickiness, and crowd out point-solution competitors. The SMB CCTV market was the highest-value adjacency: large addressable spend, fragmented supply, and a customer base that already trusts Ooredoo for mission-critical connectivity.
Strategic Frame
The commercial thesis: SMBs do not want to buy cameras — they want peace of mind, evidence on demand, and predictable cost. The right product is not a hardware sale but a fully managed subscription that bundles cameras, installation, cloud retention, maintenance, and support into a single monthly fee on the existing Ooredoo invoice. That framing converts a high-friction CAPEX decision into a low-friction OPEX one, attaches naturally to the connectivity bundle, and positions Ooredoo as the single-vendor security partner for the SMB segment.
Business Case & Revenue Model
The SMB surveillance opportunity in-market was sized in the tens of thousands of addressable sites — retail outlets, F&B locations, professional services offices, clinics, warehouses — with annual spend across hardware, installation, and storage running into the hundreds of millions of QAR and growing. Modeled the offer as a tiered monthly subscription per site, scaling with camera count, retention window, and service level. The revenue model deliberately replaced one-time hardware margin with predictable, multi-year recurring revenue: each subscribed site generated 3–5× the lifetime contribution of an equivalent one-time hardware resale, with materially higher gross margin once amortization spread across the contract term. The subscription structure also compounded ARPA with every renewal cycle, against a hardware-resale model where revenue resets to zero the day the box ships. The business case cleared the investment hurdle on the strength of recurring revenue contribution alone, before any portfolio-adjacency upside.
Target Segments & Use Cases
The launch segments were chosen for surveillance density, regulatory exposure, and existing Ooredoo-customer overlap:
  • Retail — shrink reduction, after-hours coverage, evidence on demand for insurance and incident claims
  • Hospitality (F&B, hotels) — staff and guest safety, dispute resolution, regulatory recording in licensed venues
  • Professional services & clinics — reception-area monitoring, after-hours security, compliance recording where mandated
  • Multi-site SMB operators — centralized visibility across locations, single vendor relationship, consolidated billing — the highest-LTV cohort and the strongest natural fit for the managed model
Across every segment, the offer was scoped to address surveillance and compliance requirements the customer already faced — not to upsell capability the customer did not need.
Portfolio Expansion Strategy
CCTV-as-a-Service was deliberately scoped as the first managed-security offer, not the only one. The same commercial wrapper — subscription pricing, partner-led delivery, single invoice, single account relationship — was designed to extend naturally to access control, alarm monitoring, business Wi-Fi, and managed IT services. Each adjacent offer reuses the same billing, onboarding, partner-management, and customer-success rails, so incremental launches add revenue without proportional operating cost. The strategic outcome is a broader managed-services portfolio for Ooredoo’s SMB segment, with CCTV as the anchor product that establishes the pattern and proves the unit economics.
1

Commercial Model — CAPEX-to-OPEX with ARPA Mechanics

Designed a tiered subscription model priced by camera count, retention window, and service level — mapped directly to SMB sub-segments (retail, F&B, professional services, multi-site operators). Anchored pricing to the monthly OPEX equivalent of a 36-month CAPEX purchase plus ongoing storage and maintenance, then layered margin into the managed-service wrap rather than the hardware. Built the offer to attach to the existing connectivity bill, so every CCTV sale flows through the same billing relationship, the same account manager, and the same renewal cycle — lifting ARPA without adding billing complexity for the customer.

2

Delivery Ecosystem & Service Model

Structured the supply side as a partner-led delivery model: licensed surveillance vendors deliver hardware, installation, and field maintenance; Ooredoo owns connectivity, the customer relationship, billing, and service orchestration. Negotiated partner economics that protected Ooredoo margin while giving partners predictable recurring volume — a sustainable commercial split that aligns both sides to long-term customer retention rather than one-off hardware sales. The service-delivery design — standard onboarding workflows, SLA-backed response, consolidated single-pane visibility for multi-site operators — is built on Ooredoo’s existing field-operations and SMB support tiers, so the offer scales without standing up a parallel ops organization. The result is operational scalability across site count and segment, and commercial resilience against hardware-supply or single-vendor risk because partner substitution does not break the customer experience.

3

Go-to-Market & Operational Readiness

Built the launch motion around Ooredoo’s existing SMB channels: account managers carry the offer into renewal and expansion conversations, retail stores demo it to walk-in business customers, and the digital self-service channel handles single-site low-touch purchases. Designed installation and service workflows that integrate cleanly with Ooredoo’s existing field operations and SMB support tiers — no parallel ops org required. Sales enablement, pricing tools, contract templates, partner SLAs, and customer onboarding flows are complete. The offer is operationally ready to ship the moment regulatory clearance closes.

4

Launch Governance & Regulatory Readiness

Ran the program through a single launch governance forum with named owners across Product, Legal, Regulatory Affairs, Sales, Operations, and Partner Management. Each function carried explicit launch-readiness commitments and reported against them at every milestone gate: Product owned the offer definition and pricing; Legal owned partner and customer contracts; Sales owned channel enablement and quotas; Operations owned installation, service, and support readiness; Partner Management owned licensed-vendor onboarding and SLAs. Regulatory Affairs led the Ministry of Interior approval process for the managed-service commercial model — a regulatory step specific to surveillance categories in-market — preparing the submission package, managing engagement with the regulator, and coordinating clarifications. Governance kept all six workstreams synchronized to a single launch-readiness state rather than each running its own timeline.

Commercialization Readiness Outcomes
  • Commercial model validated — subscription tiers, segment fit, and unit economics signed off against the business case
  • Pricing approved — tiered monthly pricing approved across SMB sub-segments, with attach incentives mapped to the connectivity bundle
  • Partner agreements complete — licensed surveillance vendors contracted on commercial terms, SLAs, and recurring-volume commitments
  • Sales readiness complete — enablement, battlecards, pricing tools, contract templates, and channel quotas in place across field, retail, and digital
  • Operational readiness complete — installation, provisioning, billing, service, and support workflows tested end-to-end on existing SMB ops infrastructure
  • Regulatory submission completed — full Ministry of Interior submission filed for the managed-service commercial model; clearance pending

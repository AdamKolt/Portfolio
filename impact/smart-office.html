Smart Office — Category Redesign Beats Price War

Competitive Response Category Creation Premium Positioning 5G-Native Architecture Anti-Cannibalization Controls

A low-cost challenger had captured roughly 65% of micro-SMB share in entry-level fiber connectivity through aggressive price discounting. Rather than match the challenger with a cheaper fiber SKU or exit the segment, I designed Smart Office as a different category entirely — 5G voice and data over mobile infrastructure, premium-positioned, with strict anti-cannibalization controls. The launch recovered share, protected the existing fiber base, and sold 1,500 units in Year 1 without triggering a margin-eroding price war.

Context
A low-cost challenger had captured roughly 65% of the entry-level micro-SMB fiber connectivity segment by undercutting the market on price with lower-speed fiber offers. Internally, the organization was split. One group wanted to launch a cheaper competing fiber package to defend share. Another argued the segment should be abandoned entirely because the economics were unattractive. Both sides shared the same concern: any low-priced response risked cannibalizing the existing fiber customer base and compressing margins across the portfolio.
The Decision Frame
I owned the decision on whether to respond to the challenger and, if so, the commercial strategy and product approach. The objective was to recover share in entry-level SMB without triggering a price war or damaging the economics of the existing fiber base. Instead of accepting the binary choice the organization was debating — match price or exit — I redesigned the category itself.
1

From Fiber to 5G Mobile — Different Economics, Lower Cannibalization Risk

I shifted the underlying architecture from fixed fiber to 5G mobile infrastructure. Smart Office combined voice and data services over mobile connectivity instead of fixed fiber, which eliminated fiber rollout and last-mile costs and gave the offer structurally better unit economics. Just as importantly, mobile-based connectivity created clear category separation from the existing fiber base, materially reducing the risk that existing fiber customers would substitute down into the cheaper offer.

2

Premium Positioning — Not a Race to the Bottom

I deliberately priced Smart Office above the challenger, not below. The challenger was positioned as cheap connectivity. I positioned Smart Office as a modern, reliable small-business solution with better service quality and operational simplicity. That allowed Ooredoo to compete on differentiated value rather than entering a margin-destroying price war. The premium-positioned framing also reinforced that the customer was choosing a newer operating model, not buying downgraded telecom service.

3

Modernized Experience — IP Voice Instead of Landlines

I modernized the experience itself by using IP phones instead of traditional landlines. The IP-voice substitution further reinforced the perception that Smart Office customers were adopting a newer, cleaner operating model rather than accepting a downgraded version of legacy telecom service. It also created operational simplicity advantages over the challenger’s lower-speed fiber alternative.

4

Anti-Cannibalization Controls — Enforced Through CRM, Channel, and Comp

I implemented strict anti-cannibalization controls. Smart Office was restricted to new acquisitions within the entry-level micro-SMB segment only. Existing fiber customers could not migrate into the offer. I enforced this operationally through three layers:

  • CRM eligibility rules that blocked existing fiber accounts from being quoted Smart Office
  • Channel governance that prevented field reps and partner agents from bypassing the policy
  • Commission controls that disincentivized any attempt to migrate existing customers into the lower-margin offer

The hardest part of the work was not the product design or the pricing structure. It was aligning commercial, product, and channel leaders behind a strategy that did not fit the binary options being debated internally. I had to convince stakeholders that the answer was not match price or abandon the segment, but redesign the category economics entirely.

Outcomes
  • 1,500 units sold in Year 1 — sustained acquisition product within the SMB portfolio
  • Share recovery in micro-SMB without triggering a price war
  • Zero cannibalization of the existing fiber customer base, protected by CRM, channel, and comp controls
  • Margin discipline preserved through premium positioning and structurally lower 5G economics
  • The challenger’s low-price advantage stopped translating into dominant share growth
What I Learned

Many difficult commercial decisions appear binary only because the option set is incomplete. Once we reframed the problem from "How do we respond to cheaper fiber?" to "How do we serve this segment with different economics?", a much stronger solution became visible. The category redesign was not obvious from inside the original debate. It was only visible from outside it.

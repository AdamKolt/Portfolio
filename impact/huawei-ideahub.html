Huawei IdeaHub Smart Board

CAPEX-to-OPEX Retention via Modernization Hands-On Adoption Huawei Co-Sell

Ooredoo partnered with Huawei in 2022 to commercialize the IdeaHub collaboration platform across the SMB market — a three-in-one device combining interactive whiteboarding, video conferencing, and wireless presentation. The product was valuable; the barriers were the problem. SMBs are cash-flow constrained, the device was unfamiliar, and a four-figure capex purchase was a hard ask. I redesigned the go-to-market motion by working backward from each customer barrier — commercial model, bundling, experience, and a proactive retention play — and reinforced it with internal alignment across sales, telemarketing, and compensation. Year-one: 800 units sold and Huawei’s Best Carrier Partner of the Year (MECA).

Customer Barriers
IdeaHub was a meaningful addition to any modern organization, but the barriers were clear across the SMB base. Cash-flow-constrained customers were unwilling to absorb a four-figure capex purchase of an unfamiliar device, the product category itself was new to most buyers, and the value of the three-in-one experience (whiteboard, video, wireless presentation) was hard to grasp from a spec sheet. Internally, the obvious play was to push the device as a hardware sale and let the price discount do the work. That motion was not going to scale.
The Decision Frame — Start from the Customer, Walk Backward
The objective was to make IdeaHub accessible, understandable, and commercially attractive to SMB customers without discounting the product. The question was how to remove each customer barrier that was keeping otherwise-willing SMBs from buying. I worked backward from what the customer was experiencing at each stage of the decision and designed the motion around that.
1

CAPEX → OPEX — Lease-to-Own That Fits SMB Cash Flow

I redesigned the commercial model from a capital purchase to an operating expense. Huawei financed the device; the SMB paid a predictable monthly fee that fit their cash-flow profile. The lease-to-own structure removed the single largest barrier in the buying decision — the upfront outlay — and reframed IdeaHub as a recurring service rather than a hardware acquisition.

2

Bundle with Connectivity — One Monthly Bill for the Use Case

I built bundles that combined the IdeaHub device with Ooredoo connectivity for specific use cases — smart classrooms, hospitality, executive boardrooms, and similar modern workplace scenarios. Customers received the complete solution on a single monthly invoice instead of negotiating a hardware purchase plus a connectivity contract in parallel. The bundle simplified the buying decision and positioned IdeaHub as a use-case solution rather than a standalone product.

3

Hands-On Lab — Experience Before Commit

I set up a hands-on lab where SMB customers could come and experience IdeaHub before committing. Customers walked in, used the device themselves, ran a practice meeting on it, tested the whiteboard, and saw the video conferencing quality in person. Letting prospects experience the three-in-one capability directly closed the comprehension gap that no brochure or specification sheet could close on its own.

4

Proactive Retention — Modernizing Customers Before the Market Did

While designing the GTM, I identified a win-win retention opportunity. A segment of existing customers was still operating on slower legacy connectivity plans that were more expensive than current plans. Doing nothing meant waiting for competitors to expose the value gap eventually.

Instead, I pushed the organization to approach those customers first, with a modernization offer: significantly faster connectivity, access to new collaboration technology through IdeaHub, and an overall better digital experience without increasing their monthly spend. The objective was not short-term upsell revenue. It was protecting long-term customer trust by ensuring existing customers benefited from innovation before the market forced the conversation externally.

5

Internal Alignment — Sales Motion, Telemarketing, Compensation

Customer-facing design alone is not enough. I made four internal alignments to ensure the field could sell the motion successfully:

  • Sales collateral positioning the three-in-one device as a necessity for any modern business, not a luxury — clear, simple, and outcome-led
  • Targeted telemarketing motion with fine-tuned scripts and Sales Plays for the specific SMB segments (education, hospitality, modern workplace)
  • Compensation structure that paid sellers full commission credit immediately upon sale, even though the customer paid monthly — eliminating the structural seller resistance to subscription products
  • Bundle architecture tying IdeaHub to Ooredoo connectivity services so the field sold a complete smart-classroom and collaboration solution rather than a standalone piece of hardware
Outcomes
  • 800 units sold in Year 1 across SMB segments — particularly hospitality, education, and modern workplace
  • Ooredoo Qatar recognized as Huawei’s Best Carrier Partner of the Year across the Middle East and Central Asia (MECA) region for IdeaHub sales performance in 2023
  • CAPEX-to-OPEX commercial model became a reference template for subsequent hardware-plus-service commercialization plays in the SMB portfolio
  • Customer trust preserved through proactive modernization of legacy-plan accounts, ahead of any competitive disruption
